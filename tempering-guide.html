Tempering Spices

Tempering blooms whole spices in hot fat and pours their flavour over a dish. It takes a minute and transforms a simple pot of lentils.

✦

What tempering does

Many flavour compounds in spices dissolve in fat, not water. Heating whole spices in oil or ghee draws those compounds into the fat, which then carries flavour through the whole dish when it is poured over the top or stirred in.

✦

Choose your fat

Ghee gives a rich, nutty finish; a neutral oil lets the spices lead; mustard oil adds pungency. Use enough to cover the base of a small pan, usually one to two tablespoons for a family-sized dish.

✦

Add in the right order

  1. Seeds that pop or take longest go first: mustard, then cumin or fennel.
  2. Then whole dried chillies, cloves or cinnamon.
  3. Leaves and garlic last: curry leaves, then sliced garlic.
  4. Ground spices, if any, for just a few seconds off the heat.
✦

Read the signs

Mustard seeds pop and dance; cumin sizzles and turns a shade darker; curry leaves crackle and turn glossy. If anything smells sharp or looks black, it has burnt: start again, as bitterness will spread through the dish.

✦

Where to use it

Pour a tempering over lentils, yoghurt, soups, rice or roasted vegetables just before serving. You can also begin a curry by tempering spices first, then building the rest of the dish on top.
