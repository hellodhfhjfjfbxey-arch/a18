Whole & Ground Spices

Whole spices keep their flavour locked in. Ground spices give it up quickly. Knowing which to use, and when, changes everything.

✦

Why whole lasts longer

A spice’s aroma comes from volatile oils sealed inside the seed, bark or pod. Grinding breaks those cells open and the oils start to fade. Whole spices keep for one to two years; ground spices are best within about six months.

✦

Toasting whole spices

Warm a dry, heavy pan over medium heat, add the whole spices and shake often. After 30 seconds to two minutes they darken slightly and smell fragrant. Tip them straight onto a plate so they stop cooking, and let them cool.

✦

Grinding at home

  • Mortar and pestle: best for small amounts and a coarse, textured grind.
  • Spice grinder: fast and fine; keep one just for spices.
  • Rolling pin: for cracking peppercorns or cardamom in a pinch.
✦

When to use ground

Ground spices dissolve into sauces, rubs and baking where a smooth texture matters. Add them to warm fat for a few seconds to bloom, or stir them into a sauce, but watch closely: ground spices burn far faster than whole.

✦

Storing for flavour

Keep spices in airtight jars away from the stove, sunlight and steam. Write the date on the label. Smell before you use: if the aroma is faint, use a little more, or replace the jar.
